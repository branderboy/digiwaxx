Digiwaxx vs. SubmitHub

Should I use Digiwaxx or SubmitHub?

Quick Answer

SubmitHub is a pitching marketplace: you pay small per-submission credits to reach individual blogs, curators, and labels, who must listen and respond. Digiwaxx is a record pool: one campaign services your record to 30,000+ working DJs. One buys conversations; the other buys distribution into professional crates.

SubmitHub earned its place by fixing a real problem — it forces gatekeepers to actually listen and respond, and its per-pitch pricing lets artists test cheaply. The trade-off is scale and channel: you are buying one listener at a time, mostly in the blog/playlist world. Here is how the two models compare in practice.

What Each Actually Does

  • SubmitHub: a marketplace where you spend credits (a few dollars each) to pitch individual curators, bloggers, radio hosts, and labels. Premium submissions guarantee a listen and a response — the honest-feedback mechanism is the product.
  • Digiwaxx: a record pool since 1998 — one campaign packages your record DJ-ready and services it to 30,000+ club, mixshow, and radio DJs at once, with radio rotation, playlist placement, and published coverage layered in ($99–$199 one-time).

Strengths of Each Model

SubmitHub shines for testing: cheap, fast, brutally honest feedback from real gatekeepers, useful before you spend bigger money — and for targeting specific blogs or niche playlists one by one.

The pool model shines for reach into a channel SubmitHub barely touches: working DJs. No per-DJ fee, no pitch-writing per target — the record sits in the professional crates DJs already check weekly, and reaction data comes back from actual rooms, not inbox listens.

The Practical Combination

They stack naturally: use SubmitHub-style pitching to test reception and collect quotable feedback; use DJ service to create real-world activity; then pitch curators anywhere with the receipts DJ activity generates. The mistake is treating either as the whole campaign — one is a focus group and a door-knocker, the other is distribution into the rooms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SubmitHub worth it for hip hop and club genres?

As a feedback tool, yes. As a breaking mechanism, club genres rarely break through blog pitches — they break through rooms, which is the DJ channel. Use each for what it is.

Why is per-DJ pitching not how DJ promotion works?

Because DJs discover records through pools and peer crates, not inboxes — the professional channel pre-packages and pre-filters records so DJs can trust and use them instantly. That is the infrastructure a pool campaign buys.
