Digiwaxx vs. Playlist Push

Should I use Digiwaxx or Playlist Push to promote my song?

Quick Answer

Different categories: Playlist Push pitches your song to independent playlist curators and TikTok creators; Digiwaxx services your record to 30,000+ club, mixshow, and radio DJs. Playlists scale streams for a moving record; DJs create the real-world reaction that gets records moving.

This is not a "we win, they lose" page — Playlist Push is a legitimate, well-known platform, and plenty of artists use both channels. The real question is sequencing: which exposure does your record need right now, streaming-playlist distribution or real-world DJ reaction? Here is the honest breakdown.

What Each Actually Does

  • Playlist Push: a campaign platform that pitches your song to networks of independent playlist curators (and, in its creator product, TikTok creators), who review and optionally add/use it. Campaign budgets typically run into the hundreds of dollars, priced by reach.
  • Digiwaxx: a record pool operating since 1998 — your record packaged DJ-ready and serviced to 30,000+ working DJs (club, mixshow, radio), plus radio rotation, playlist placement, and published artist coverage in one one-time-fee campaign ($99–$199).

When Playlist-First Makes Sense

If your record is a streaming-native lane — chill, lo-fi, singer-songwriter, mood music that lives in headphones rather than rooms — playlist campaigns target exactly where your listeners are, and DJ rooms may never be the natural habitat. Curator campaigns also make sense as a second wave for any record that already has motion and receipts.

When DJ-First Makes Sense

If your record is hip hop, R&B, afrobeats, dancehall, Latin, or anything built for rooms, the DJ layer is where records in your genre actually break — and playlist pitches convert far better once there is real-world reaction to point to. DJ service also produces assets playlists cannot: club clips, spin history, DJ relationships, and the search/Shazam activity that wakes up algorithmic playlists on its own.

The Honest Sequencing

For club-genre records: DJ service first, curator campaigns second, with proof attached. For streaming-native records: playlist campaigns first, and consider DJs only if a remix or edit gives the record a room life. Using both in that order is a strategy; using either as your only channel is a bet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run Digiwaxx and Playlist Push at the same time?

Yes, and for club-genre records the smart order is DJ service slightly ahead — curator response rates improve when your pitch carries spins, clips, and momentum.

Which is cheaper?

Digiwaxx campaigns are one-time $99–$199. Playlist Push campaigns are budget-based and typically cost more; check their current pricing. Cheaper isn’t the question though — channel fit is.
