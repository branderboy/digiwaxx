The 30-Day Post-Release Sprint

How do I run a real campaign on a song that is already released?

Quick Answer

Days 1 to 5: fix packaging and submit to DJ service. Days 6 to 19: run a two-week content burst around one hook moment while DJs test the record. Days 20 to 30: pitch curators and mixshows with the proof the sprint produced, and book the plan for wave two.

This is the campaign for the most common situation in independent music: the song is out, the launch was quiet, and you want a structured way to give it a real shot. It runs 30 days, needs no re-upload, and is built around one principle, a released record does not need another platform; it needs people. Put real dates on it and start.

Days 1 to 5: Repair and Service

  • Day 1: audit the record like a stranger, clean version? instrumental? correct tags? artwork that reads professional at thumbnail size?
  • Days 2 to 3: fix what failed the audit; export clean, dirty, and instrumental at full quality
  • Day 4: submit for DJ service, pools take released records every day; DJs care whether it works in a room, not when it dropped
  • Day 5: set your baseline: screenshot current streams, saves, listeners, and sources so you can measure the sprint honestly

Days 6 to 19: The Burst

  • Pick one moment: the 10 to 15 seconds of the song people react to, every post in the burst uses it
  • Post daily or near-daily for two weeks: performance clips, the story behind the record, any DJ activity the service produces
  • Days 8 to 12: personal outreach round, 20 to 50 individual messages to real supporters with a direct link
  • Every DJ download, spin, or comment gets amplified same-day, tag the DJ, thank them publicly, save every clip to your proof kit

Days 20 to 30: Pitch With Receipts

  • Days 20 to 23: pitch independent curators, two sentences, the strongest clip or stat from the burst, direct link
  • Days 24 to 26: pitch mixshows and college radio in any market where the record showed life; clean version and one-sheet attached
  • Days 27 to 29: compare against your Day-5 baseline: sources, cities, save rate. Decide wave two, video, remix, or doubling down on the one city that moved
  • Day 30: schedule wave two. A record with two waves behind it is a campaign; a record with one is an attempt.

Frequently Asked Questions

My song has been out for months, does this still apply?

Yes. DJs, curators, and algorithms respond to current activity, not release dates. A six-month-old record with fresh spins and content is a current record.

What results should I expect in 30 days?

Honest answer: movement, not miracles, DJ downloads and feedback, first spins, measurable source changes in Spotify for Artists, and a proof kit that makes every next pitch stronger. Records compound across waves, not weeks.
