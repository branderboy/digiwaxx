Should I Pay for Music Promotion?

Should I pay to promote my music?

Quick Answer

Pay when three things are true: the record is genuinely finished (mastered, clean versions ready), the money buys real access (DJs, radio, curators, not bots), and you can work the results (content, follow-ups). If the record isn’t packaged yet, spend there first.

The honest answer is a checklist, not a yes/no. Promotion multiplies what exists, a great record properly packaged gets multiplied; an unmastered file with no profiles gets multiplied by zero. Here is the readiness test, and the decision logic on the other side of it.

The Readiness Checklist

  • Mastered audio that survives a big system
  • Clean, dirty, and instrumental versions exported
  • Consistent, claimed profiles everywhere (a stranger who searches you finds you)
  • Somewhere for new attention to land, link-in-bio, a follow-worthy profile, a capture point
  • Time to work the campaign: post reactions, thank DJs, pitch with receipts

Pass all five and paid promotion buys leverage. Fail any and that's where your first dollars go instead.

What to Pay For (In Order)

Access beats impressions: DJ service puts your record in front of 30,000+ working DJs, exposure you cannot DM your way into. Then legitimate pitching with the proof that service generates, then ads only on records with demonstrated reaction. Never pay for guaranteed streams, spins, or placements, real audiences can't be pre-sold, and fake ones damage you.

The Free Work That Makes Paid Work

Paid exposure converts through free labor: content around the record, replies to every comment, personal outreach to supporters, and relationships with the DJs and curators who respond. Artists who "paid for promotion and got nothing" usually bought the exposure and skipped the conversion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it bad to pay for promotion as a new artist?

No, every label in history has paid for promotion; it is how records reach strangers. What is bad is paying before the record is packaged, or paying for fake numbers instead of access.

What is the minimum sensible spend?

Roughly $100 to $300 per single covers packaging gaps plus a DJ campaign, the two highest-leverage purchases. Below that, spend time instead: content, outreach, and the free halves of every channel.
