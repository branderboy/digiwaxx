What Is a Record Pool?

What is a record pool?

Quick Answer

A record pool is a membership-based service that distributes new music from artists and labels to verified, working DJs, packaged with the clean versions, edits, and metadata DJs need, and returns feedback on what DJs are actually playing.

The term comes from 1970s New York, when club DJs "pooled" together so labels could service one organization instead of hundreds of individuals, the first pools were central to how disco records broke. The model digitized but never changed shape: pools remain the professional channel between new records and the DJs who play them, sitting alongside distributors (for stores) and promoters (for press) in the release stack.

The Definition, Unpacked

  • Membership-based: DJs join and are verified as working DJs, that curation is the product
  • DJ-ready packaging: clean/dirty/instrumental versions, correct tags and BPM, files a professional can drop straight into a set
  • Two-way: music flows to DJs; feedback, ratings, and spin activity flow back to the artist side

A Short History

1975: the first record pool forms in New York so labels can efficiently service club DJs. Pools become the engine room of disco, then hip hop and dance music, the place where records get tested before radio commits. Through the CD and MP3 eras the pools go digital; today a pool services a national network in hours. Digiwaxx entered in 1998, built around hip hop and R&B, and now reaches 30,000+ DJs.

Why It Matters to an Independent Artist

The pool is the one piece of label infrastructure an independent can rent on day one. You cannot cold-call thousands of DJs, but you can be in the crate they check every week. For a genre that breaks through rooms, hip hop, R&B, afrobeats, dance, that access is the difference between a record that exists and a record that gets tested.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can join a record pool?

On the DJ side, working DJs who pass verification. On the music side, labels and independent artists submitting properly packaged records.

Are record pools legal?

Yes, pools operate with promotional rights granted by the artists and labels submitting music. They are a standard, decades-old part of industry promotion.
