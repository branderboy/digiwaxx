How Do DJs Find New Music?

How do DJs find new music?

Quick Answer

DJs find new music through record pools (the professional supply line), label promo lists, peer DJ networks, digging platforms and stores, and audience-demand tools like Shazam and charts. The mix varies by DJ type — but pools anchor nearly all of them.

Different DJs dig differently: a club DJ needs this weekend’s reactors, a mixshow jock needs exclusives and clean edits, a mobile DJ needs breadth across decades and formats, and an open-format DJ needs a bit of everything. But across all of them, the professional channels share one property: records arrive pre-packaged, pre-filtered, and ready to play. That is the bar your record has to clear.

The Channels, Ranked by Reliance

  1. Record pools — the standing subscription for working DJs; new records in every format, correctly tagged, every week
  2. Peer networks — crews, group chats, and watching other DJs' rooms; the trust layer
  3. Label promo lists — direct servicing for established relationships (the thing pools democratize)
  4. Stores & digging platforms — Beatport, Bandcamp, specialty stores, especially in dance lanes
  5. Demand tools — Shazam charts, streaming charts, TikTok sounds: what audiences are starting to request

What All Channels Have in Common

Curation and packaging. Every channel a DJ trusts filters out unfinished, mistagged, single-version records before the DJ ever sees them. The practical lesson for artists: the way into DJ discovery is not louder outreach — it is meeting professional packaging standards and being present in the filtered channels, starting with the pool.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do DJs pay for record pools?

Yes — DJs pay subscription fees for pool access, which is exactly why they check them: they are paying for a curated supply line and use what they pay for.

How often do DJs look for new music?

Working DJs refresh crates weekly — often before every weekend. Consistent presence in their channels beats one-time blasts.

Where do radio DJs find new music?

Mixshow and radio DJs pull from the same pools and peer networks as club DJs, plus label servicing and their station’s programming. Club reaction is often what earns a record its first mixshow look.
