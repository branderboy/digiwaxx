How Record Pools Work

How does a record pool actually work?

Quick Answer

A record pool is a distribution service between music and working DJs: artists and labels submit records in DJ-ready form; verified DJs download what fits their rooms; feedback and spin activity flow back. One submission reaches thousands of DJs at once.

Record pools solved a physical problem in the 1970s — labels couldn’t press and mail vinyl to every club DJ, so DJs organized into "pools" that received records centrally. The physics changed; the economics didn’t. Labels and artists still need one pipe to thousands of DJs, and DJs still need a trusted, curated source of properly packaged new records. The modern pool is that pipe, digital and instant.

The Flow, Step by Step

  1. Submission: the artist/label delivers the record with DJ-ready packaging — clean, dirty, and instrumental versions, correct metadata (artist, title, BPM, genre)
  2. Curation & servicing: the pool formats and pushes the record to its DJ membership, organized so DJs find what fits their format
  3. DJ pickup: club, mixshow, mobile, and radio DJs download and test records in their rooms and shows
  4. Feedback loop: download counts, DJ ratings, and spin reports flow back — real reaction data from working professionals

Why DJs Are in the Pool

Membership is the DJ's supply line: a steady feed of new, correctly tagged, high-quality files across their formats, before or as records hit the public. Pools verify that members actually work — which is what makes the network valuable to the artist side. Digiwaxx's network has grown since 1998 to 30,000+ working DJs.

What the Artist Gets

  • Reach: thousands of professional crates from one submission
  • Real-world testing: rooms, not dashboards, judge the record
  • Proof: DJ feedback and spins become ammunition for radio, playlist, and booking pitches
  • Speed: the whole network is serviced in days, not the years it takes to build DJ relationships one by one

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a record pool different from a distributor?

A distributor (DistroKid, TuneCore, etc.) puts your song on streaming stores for listeners. A record pool puts your song in the hands of working DJs for play in clubs, mixshows, and radio. They are complementary — most serious releases use both.

Do record pools accept independent artists?

Yes — modern pools service independent records alongside label releases. What matters is professional packaging: mastered audio, clean edits, and correct metadata.

What should I submit to a record pool?

Clean, dirty, and instrumental versions at high quality, final artwork, and accurate metadata. Radio-ready packaging maximizes which DJs can actually use the record.
