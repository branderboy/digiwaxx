How to Release a Single

What is the right way to release a single as an independent artist?

Quick Answer

Finish your master, register your ISRC and splits, upload to a distributor 3–4 weeks early, pitch playlists, build a 2-week teaser run, and service the record to DJs before release day — so the song lands with an audience waiting.

Most independent releases fail before they come out, because the artist treats the upload as the release. The upload is paperwork. The release is everything you line up around it: the distributor lead time, the playlist pitch window, the content you post, and — the part almost everyone skips — putting the record in the hands of people who play music for crowds every night. Here is the order of operations that working artists follow.

Step 1: Lock the Assets Before You Touch a Distributor

You need a mastered WAV (clean and explicit versions if the record has language), 3000×3000 artwork, and your metadata decided: final title, artist name spelled exactly the way it appears everywhere else, producer credits, and songwriter splits agreed in writing. Changing metadata after distribution is slow and can reset your playlist eligibility.

If you plan to work the record to DJs — and you should — also keep an instrumental and a clean edit ready. DJs and mixshows can't touch a record that only exists as one explicit file.

Step 2: Distribute 3–4 Weeks Out

Upload to your distributor at least three weeks before release day. That buys you time to fix errors, lets the song hit Spotify for Artists so you can pitch the editorial team (which requires a minimum lead time), and gives pre-save campaigns room to breathe.

  • 4 weeks out: distribution live in the backend, editorial pitch submitted.
  • 3 weeks out: pre-save link out, first teaser content posted.
  • 2 weeks out: DJ service begins — get the record to record pools and DJ networks so it's warm before the public can even stream it.
  • Release week: daily content, every link updated, ask lists ready.

Step 3: Put People Behind the Record, Not Just Platforms

An upload gives you availability. It does not give you an audience. The records that move after release day are the ones that had humans committed to playing them: club DJs, mixshow DJs, radio jocks, playlist curators. This is exactly what a record pool exists for — Digiwaxx has been servicing records to its DJ network since 1998, and that network now reaches 30,000+ DJs.

Servicing DJs before release day means your song can get club tests, mixshow spins, and real reactions in week one — the same week the algorithms are deciding whether your song deserves attention.

Step 4: Release Day Is a Work Day

On release day: confirm the song is live on every DSP, update every link in every bio, post the content you prepared (don't create it that day), personally message your core supporters with a direct link, and thank every DJ, curator, and fan who shares it. Then keep going — week two matters more than day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I plan a single release?

Six to eight weeks is comfortable. The hard minimum is about three weeks, driven by distributor processing time and the Spotify editorial pitch window. Anything shorter and you are choosing to skip playlist pitching and pre-release DJ service.

Do I need a clean version of my single?

If the record has explicit content, yes. Radio cannot play it and most club and mixshow DJs will not touch it without a clean edit. Delivering clean, dirty, and instrumental versions is standard when servicing DJs.

Should I release the single before or after the video?

Release the song first and let the video be a second wave two to four weeks later. That gives the record two news moments instead of one.
