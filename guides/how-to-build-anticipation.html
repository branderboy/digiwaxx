How to Build Anticipation Before a Release

How do you build real anticipation before releasing a song?

Quick Answer

Anticipation comes from repetition plus scarcity: tease one moment of the song repeatedly (not the whole record), give people one action to take (pre-save), and let tastemakers — DJs, hosts, curators — have it early so strangers hear about it from voices they trust.

Tease the Hook, Not the Song

Pick the 10–15 seconds people will rap back at you, and use that same moment across every teaser. Familiarity is the goal: by release day the hook should already feel known. Posting the whole song early kills the reason to show up on release day; posting five different snippets splits attention five ways.

One Ask: Pre-Save

Every teaser gets the same call to action. Pre-saves convert to day-one streams, and day-one velocity is what the algorithm reads. Do not ask people to follow, share, comment, and pre-save — pick the one that counts.

Let Tastemakers Have It First

The strongest anticipation is hearing a record you cannot stream yet. Service the record to DJs one to two weeks before release: club tests and mixshow spins before street date make the release an event ("out Friday" means something when people already heard it in a room). This is how labels have built release-day demand for decades, and a record pool gives an independent artist the same mechanism.

The Two-Week Countdown

  • Day −14: announce with artwork + date; pre-save live
  • Day −14 to −10: record serviced to DJs
  • Day −7: hook teaser #2, behind-the-song story
  • Day −3: repost any early DJ spins; final teaser
  • Day −1: "tomorrow" post; personal reminders to your core supporters

Frequently Asked Questions

How early should I announce a release?

Two weeks of public countdown is plenty for a single — long enough to build familiarity, short enough to hold attention. Projects and albums can run four to six weeks.

Do pre-saves actually matter?

Yes — they convert into first-day streams and library adds, which feed the early velocity signals streaming algorithms use to decide whether to push a record further.
