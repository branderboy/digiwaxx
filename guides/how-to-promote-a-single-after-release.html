How to Promote a Single After Release

How do you promote a single that is already released?

Quick Answer

A released single is a live asset — promote it in waves: service it to DJs now (pools take released records), pitch independent curators with any traction, ship a new asset (video or remix) as a news moment, and concentrate content in 2–3 week bursts.

Plenty of guides assume you have six weeks of runway before release day. Real life: the song is out, the launch was quiet, and you want to know what still works. Good news — almost everything except Spotify editorial pitching works just as well after release, and some things (pitching with real data) work better.

What You Can Still Do (Almost Everything)

  • DJ service: record pools service released records every day — DJs care whether it works in a room, not when it dropped
  • Independent curators: pitch with the numbers and clips you have; a record with any live data is an easier pitch than an unreleased promise
  • Mixshow and college radio: spins go to records that are reacting, whenever they came out
  • Content: the song's story, live clips, reactions — the feed does not know your release date

The only real casualty of a missed pre-release window is editorial playlist pitching — and editorial was never the make-or-break channel for independent records anyway.

The 30-Day Post-Release Sprint

  • Days 1–5: package properly (clean/dirty/instrumental), submit to DJ service, fix any metadata issues
  • Days 5–15: content burst around one hook moment; personal outreach to your core; watch for DJ feedback
  • Days 15–30: pitch curators and mixshows with everything the first two weeks produced; book anything bookable; plan wave two (video or remix)

Re-Release Only as a Last Resort

Do not take the record down to "relaunch" it. You lose counts, adds, and history. If the original upload is genuinely broken (wrong master, wrong name), fix and move on. Otherwise: same record, new wave.

Frequently Asked Questions

How late is too late to promote a single?

There is no expiry. Records have broken a year after release because a DJ, a playlist, or a clip finally connected. The record is promotable as long as you are willing to work it.

Can I still get playlist adds after release?

Independent curator and algorithmic playlists, absolutely — both respond to current traction. Only the official editorial pitch is pre-release.
