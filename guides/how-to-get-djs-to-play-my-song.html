How to Get DJs to Play Your Song

How do I get real DJs to play my song in clubs and on radio?

Quick Answer

Give DJs what they need: clean, dirty, and instrumental versions with correct metadata, delivered through channels they already check — record pools and DJ networks. A properly serviced record reaches thousands of DJs; a random DM reaches zero.

DJs want new records — finding music is their job. What they do not want is chasing files, downloading from sketchy links, or editing your explicit-only MP3 themselves. The artists who get played remove every ounce of friction. That is the entire logic of the record pool: one upload, professionally packaged, delivered to thousands of working DJs who came there specifically to find new music to play.

Why DMing DJs One-by-One Fails

A club DJ gets dozens of unsolicited songs a week from strangers. Untagged files, missing clean versions, Google Drive links that expire. Even a DJ who likes your record will not spend twenty minutes fixing your delivery. Individual outreach works for relationships you already have — it does not scale to the hundreds of DJs a record needs.

What a Serviced Record Looks Like

  • Versions: clean, dirty, instrumental — minimum. Acapellas and edits are a bonus DJs love.
  • Quality: 320kbps MP3 or WAV, properly mastered.
  • Metadata: artist, title, features, BPM, genre — filled in correctly so it files right in a DJ library.
  • Delivery: through a pool or network DJs already use, so playing your record costs them zero effort.

The Record Pool Route

Record pools exist because labels needed one pipe to thousands of DJs. Digiwaxx has been that pipe since 1998 — records serviced through the network reach 30,000+ club, mixshow, and radio DJs, with real DJ feedback coming back the other way. For an independent artist, it compresses years of DJ relationship-building into a single submission.

After servicing, work the reactions: thank DJs who spin it, repost their clips, and build direct relationships with the ones who respond. The pool opens the door; you keep it open.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay DJs to play my song?

No, and paying individual DJs for club spins is a bad deal. DJs play records that work in their rooms. Your job is delivery and packaging: get a properly serviced record in front of enough DJs and the ones whose crowds respond will keep playing it for free.

What is a record pool?

A record pool is a service that distributes new music to a network of verified, working DJs, packaged with the versions and metadata DJs need, and collects feedback on what they are playing. Digiwaxx is one of the longest-running record pools in the game, live since 1998.

How many DJs does it take to break a record?

There is no magic number, but breadth beats depth early: you want many DJs testing the record in many rooms to find where it reacts. That is why servicing thousands of DJs at once outperforms convincing three DJs individually.
