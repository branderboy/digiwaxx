Music Promotion for Beginners

How does a beginner start promoting their music?

Quick Answer

Start with three moves: make your profiles findable and consistent, put out a properly packaged release (clean versions, real artwork, correct metadata), and get the record in front of people who play music for a living, DJs, curators, hosts, instead of shouting into your own feed.

Music promotion is not one thing, it is a stack: distribution makes you available, content makes you visible, and relationships (DJs, radio, curators, press) make you heard by audiences you do not already own. Beginners usually over-invest in content aimed at their existing followers and never touch the relationship layer, which is where new fans actually come from.

The Three Layers of Promotion

  • Availability: distribution to DSPs, claimed artist profiles, one link that goes everywhere. Table stakes.
  • Visibility: content around the record, clips, performances, the story of the song. This warms up people who already found you.
  • Amplification: other people with audiences playing your record, club DJs, mixshow and radio DJs, playlist curators, blogs. This is how strangers find you, and it is the layer beginners skip because it feels out of reach.

What to Ignore

  • Botted streams and fake playlist farms, they poison your data and can get your music removed from DSPs
  • Follower-count vanity, 500 real fans in one city beats 50,000 dead followers
  • "Going viral" as a plan, virality is an outcome of records reacting in the real world, not a strategy

Your First 30 Days

Week 1: claim and match every profile; write the metadata sheet for your best song. Week 2: get clean, dirty, and instrumental versions done; finalize artwork. Week 3: distribute with lead time and pitch editorial. Week 4: service the record to DJs through a record pool, this is the amplification layer, available to you on day one. Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start promoting music?

Less than you think, if it goes to the right things: distribution, clean edits, and DJ service come in well under the cost of a single mid-tier ad campaign, and they build assets (relationships, spin history) that ads never do.

Should beginners hire a PR company?

Not first. PR amplifies a story; a first release usually does not have one yet. Build DJ activity and real listener reaction first, that becomes the story a publicist can sell later.

What is the biggest beginner mistake in music promotion?

Treating the upload as the promotion. Distribution makes you available to listeners; it does nothing to bring listeners to you. Amplification through DJs, curators, and radio is what creates discovery.
